[PROJECT 04 :: CONSTRUCTION AI APPLICATION]

Separate Cloud Identities for Two Coordination Jobs

Project 04 built and tested separate AWS identities for two jobs representing upload coordination and analysis coordination. Each job entered its own role and read its own harmless settings. Requests to enter the other role, read the other configuration or list secrets received the expected denials. AWS CloudTrail records were correlated to the actual runs and targets.

In a construction video/AI scenario, wearable-camera footage may move through cloud upload and analysis stages. Those stages can have different responsibilities and should have deliberately assigned access. This independent exploration models the supporting job-identity boundary with configuration reads, making the access decisions easy to inspect and test.

Progress from Project 03: the familiar two-worker admission and access pattern is now applied to a concrete industry workflow, with an attack-oriented review and a receiving-engineer handoff. The advance is application and evidence discipline; the technical pattern and selected checks remain comparable to Project 03.

[IMPLEMENTED ARCHITECTURE :: ADMISSION THEN ACCESS]

A Job Identity Is Not a Shared Permission Pool

The upload-coordination job and analysis-coordination job have different GitHub repositories, IAM roles and Secrets Manager secrets. GitHub OIDC supplies the workload assertion. AWS STS checks whether that identity may enter the requested role; the resulting session then uses the role’s effective permissions to request configuration.

[SCOPE & TESTED LESSONS]

A Useful Cloud Boundary with a Clear Next Step

Scope: this independent lab implements CI/workload role admission and harmless configuration access for two construction-AI coordination jobs. Camera identity, footage ingestion, model protection and production operation are proposed connections, not implemented components.

The demonstrated capability is concrete: two intended jobs obtained separate temporary roles, accessed their assigned settings and were denied the specified cross-boundary requests. Applying that familiar pattern to an industry scenario makes the access responsibilities and receiving-team questions explicit.

Engineering lessons and retained responsibilities

Initial setup and the diagnostic interface caused real operator friction. Steve reported that Low/Light felt smoother and later work flowed smoothly, but the record does not establish an exact setting-switch point or a causal speed improvement. The PCH-04 experience/counter-interface issue remains OPEN; attempt allocation and attempt-four enforcement were not verified. Technical success is separate from that unresolved product finding.

The records retain eight diagnostic pairs/candidates and four completed local functional recovery invocations. A wholly rejected-before-body submission and a separate mixed terminal/apply submission are different, overlapping categories; they are not added as a synthetic retry total. Zero breaker activations and zero observed paired-ID drift do not establish counter correctness. The selected v2.2.1 source was unchanged; the frozen v2.2.2 candidate was unselected and unexecuted.

Mutable @v4 actions and the authorized job’s own access remain part of the review surface. The evidence covers selected requests; comprehensive policy-path review, immutable dependency provenance and raw-log integrity validation are separate work. No cryptographic/log-integrity certification, formal proof or universal exclusivity follows from these results.

The two roles/configurations remain retained for review on 23 November 2026. No automated cleanup was configured. Future source, trust, permission or asset changes need fresh review and tests; v2.2.2 was not adopted as a post-run update.
